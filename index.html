Goal: by mid-February, Grace can find her way around Blender unaided and has her first finished render on the shelf. Rhythm: two or three 45-minute sessions a week - she drives, you sit beside her.

  1. Install & set up (one evening)

    Download the free LTS version from blender.org. Then set up the project folder the same evening: The-Stable/2027-01-bootcamp/ with subfolders renders/, references/, wip/. Naming habits on day one save heartache in 2030.

    blender.org/downloadOfficial manual (bookmark this)
  2. Learn the room (week 1)

    Grant Abbitt's Blender 5 Complete Beginners Guide, Part 1: The Interface. Watch with Blender open, pausing to copy every move. Thirty minutes hands-on beats three hours watching.

    Grant Abbitt: Blender 5 Beginners Guide Pt 1
  3. Mouse gymnastics (weeks 1-2)

    Viewport navigation until it is boring: orbit, pan, zoom without thinking. Then the ten keyboard moves that matter: G grab, R rotate, S scale, Tab edit mode, X delete, Shift+A add, F3 search, numpad 1/3/7 for views, Z wireframe, Ctrl+Z undo (the most important one).

  4. The donut (weeks 2-5)

    The rite of passage: Blender Guru's Donut Tutorial, 2026 edition, made for Blender 5. Do parts 1-4 properly; frustration is part of the curriculum. Do not chase the whole series this half-term - level 1-2 skills are the goal.

    Donut Part 1 (2026)Whole donut in one video
  5. Fly solo (week 6)

    No tutorial allowed: model something simple from the stable - a hay bale, a bucket, a fence post. Ugly is completely fine. This is the real milestone: proof the skills transferred from the video into her hands.

  6. Ship it (final week)

    Light the scene with a Sun lamp, press F12, save the render to renders/. That is portfolio artifact zero. Screenshot the wireframe and clay versions too - the "making of" shots are portfolio gold later.

Dad notes
  • Stop while it is still fun. Ending on a win brings her back tomorrow.
  • Resist fixing it for her. "What do you think that button does?" is the whole pedagogy.
  • If the donut gets boring, swap in Grant Abbitt's series - same skills, different flavour. Consistency beats loyalty.
  • Keep everything, including failures. The ugly early renders are the before-photos of the whole journey.
